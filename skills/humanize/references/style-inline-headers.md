## Avoid Mechanical Inline-Header Lists

AI outputs lists where items start with bolded headers followed by colons. It often reads like a slide deck.

Note: inline headers aren't always wrong. In blog posts and docs, a short labeled framework can be helpful for skimming. The goal is to avoid the generic, corporate pattern—not to flatten every labeled list.

**AI pattern:**
> - **User Experience:** The user experience has been significantly improved with a new interface.
> - **Performance:** Performance has been enhanced through optimized algorithms.
> - **Security:** Security has been strengthened with end-to-end encryption.

**Human alternative:**
> The update improves the interface, speeds up load times through optimized algorithms, and adds end-to-end encryption.

**When to keep them:**
- The labels are a real framework (e.g., a small set of checks or steps).
- The labels carry meaning and improve scanning.

**Fix:**
- If the list is generic and corporate, convert to prose.
- If the list is useful, keep the structure but simplify: drop the bold, drop the colon, and keep each item tight.

Example (keep the structure, less AI-ish):
> Purpose: what job does the animation do?
> Frequency: how often will it trigger?
> Pattern: entering, exiting, progress, hover.
